In autumn 2025, a bicycle rode through four Dutch places, from a village to a city, carrying three cameras. One saw colour. One saw near-infrared, where living plants glow. And one saw heat.

We asked a simple question. Are greener streets cooler?

Every photo became a dot: how green the view was, against how warm the street's surfaces were, compared with streets ridden in the same hour.

The answer was smaller than we expected. In the town and the small city, greener streets ran slightly cooler, about a quarter of a degree for every ten percent more green in view. In the big city and the village, once we accounted for which way the bike faced the sun, there was no clear difference.

It was October. Shade does most of its work in summer.

So, in those two places, we marked the six warm, bare streets where a single tree would likely help most.
